The entrance lobby band and ADD area featured cantilevering bands extending over 3 meters. To facilitate manual han-
dling, these bands were subdivided into smaller sections. Each piece was uniquely labeled in the fabrication drawings, 
accompanied by isometric assembly diagrams to ensure accurate construction.
All shop drawing pieces were nest-
ed onto an 8’×4’ MDF sheet (8mm 
thick) for efficient material utili-
zation. The bands were sectioned 
in two directions, maintaining a 
150mm spacing between cuts. Once 
assembled, these sections formed 
the framework for GRC/FRP mold 
preparation. While molds for typical 
floors were reused, the remaining 
scissor bands were uniquely fabri-
cated. Interlocking grooves in the 
sections ensured precise alignment 
and seamless assembly.

Globally there is an ongoing climate crisis. Construction contributes 40% of global emissions and generates significant 
waste. One key strategy is reuse of salvaged materials especially timber. However, reclaimed timber introduces major 
challenges due to irregular geometry and uncertain structural performance. This raises a key question:How can we sys-
tematically use reclaimed timber in structurally informed design, while also enabling reuse and disassembly?
Research Question: This project explores how structural stress fields can guide the aggregation of reclaimed timber into 
reversible assemblies, and how graph-based representations can enable disassembly intelligence

ARWAD TOWER, JEDDAH
Project:3
This project is a commercial office 
space located near the airport in Jeddah. 
Designed for flexibility, each floor features 
office spaces available for subletting, al-
lowing different companies to set up their 
administrative offices with adaptable 
internal partitions. 
The building integrates various functional 
and sustainable design elements, includ-
ing a full-height atrium, open terraces, 
and informal meeting spaces. The first 
two floors are dedicated to commercial 
use, enhancing the building’s mixed-use 
character. Additionally, the façade design 
minimizes total heat gain, reducing 
cooling loads and improving energy 
efficiency.
Jeddah experiences high temperatures 
throughout the year, often exceeding 
45°C during working hours in the sum-
mer months. This highlights the need 
for effective shading on the facade.
From the massing catalog, Option 1 was selected 
as it covered the maximum BUA while allowing 
ample daylight to enter through the atrium. The only 
requirement was a façade design that could mitigate 
the harsh sunlight.

Machine Learning Model for Print Path Optimisation
The project establishes a parametric relation-
ship between task, material, and fabrication 
constraints to guide robotic toolpath planning. 
Print parameters such as bed size and depo-
sition strategy define geometric limitations, 
while material behaviour—particularly overhang 
tolerance and compression capacity—directly 
influences structural stability. 
 
Zoning strategies are introduced to distribute 
material based on structural demand, transi-
tioning from heavier deposition in high-stress 
regions to lighter infill in non-critical areas. This 
enables efficient material usage while maintain-
ing structural integrity. 
 
The integration of these parameters allows the 
generation of adaptive toolpaths, where geome-
try, material behaviour, and fabrication logic are 
resolved simultaneously through computational 
control.

CONFIGURABLE TOPOLOGIES
Project:5
The thesis project is about building a 
recommendation system or a digital aid to designers 
that can help them classify 3D prototypes of 
their building solutions based on their topological 
relationships. This research thesis focuses on using 
graph machine learning system that learns from its 
architectural precedents based on its topological 
forms. This conceptual workflow consist of three 
primary steps. First one is to construct synthetic 
dataset based on its architectural precedents. 
These synthetic datasets are generated using 
parametric modelling in grasshopper. Second is to 
encode graphs in the dataset. And the final step is 
to use supervised learning for graphs using Graph 
Convolution Networks (GCN). 
Dataset for 3 different typologies was created. After 
generating the dataset the graphs need to undergo 
the process of one-hot encoding where the nodes 
are labelled according to the category they belong. 
For the pupose of machine learning the nodes need 
to have some numbers associated to it. So the 
graphs undergo the closeness centrality analysis.
