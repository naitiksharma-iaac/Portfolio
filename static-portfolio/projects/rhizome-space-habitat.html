Rhizome is a collaborative proposal for a long-term settlement in Earth’s lower orbit. Instead of designing one finished space station, the group designed a system that can grow from six to 148 inhabitants through a repeatable kit of living, working, greenhouse, recreation and infrastructure modules.

Its cutting-edge premise is a computationally controlled habitat: modular geometry, shared BIM information and rule-based aggregation allow the station to expand while maintaining structural, environmental and logistical relationships. The project treats infrastructure as public space, so connectors, services and storage also become places for collective life.

A habitat that grows by rules

The project begins with orbital context, payload limits, radiation, thermal conditions, life-support requirements, energy and the operational constraints of building beyond Earth. A common modular language based on space-filling platonic geometry lets the group test different configurations without losing the system’s underlying logic.

From parametric model to live configurator

Rhino and Grasshopper were used to develop the modular geometry, connectors and growth rules. Revit and BIM workflows organised the architectural information and production drawings. The aggregation model was then connected to a web configurator, allowing alternative station arrangements to be explored as a responsive system rather than as isolated renderings.

Collaborative digital workflow

The group divided work by tasks and kept a shared communication loop so that spatial requirements, design considerations, infrastructure, axonometric views and detailed drawings could move between team members. Our submission combines the computational model, BIM documentation, web application and presentation material into one coordinated orbit infrastructure proposal.
