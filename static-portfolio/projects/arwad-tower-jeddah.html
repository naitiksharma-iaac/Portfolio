Arwad Tower is a commercial office development situated in the strategic northern growth corridor of Jeddah near King Abdulaziz International Airport. The project addresses the dual demands of high-density commercial real estate—maximizing built-up area and leasing adaptability—while confronting Jeddah’s extreme coastal desert climate, where solar exposure and heat gain shape the façade design. The design method links an eight-option parametric massing catalogue, Daylight Factor simulations, flexible multi-tenant floorplate planning, and a biomimetic phyllotactic self-shading envelope into an integrated performance-driven architecture.

01 / Urban Context & Visibility

Positioning along northern Jeddah’s transit corridor

The site occupies a pivotal position in northern Jeddah, bounded by rapid transit corridors, major expressway interchanges, and primary arterial links connecting the city centre with King Abdulaziz International Airport. This context imposes dual perceptual criteria: the building must project a strong volumetric identity at highway speed when seen from elevated motorway overpasses, while offering a welcoming, human-scaled pedestrian environment along the local street network.

The architectural language synthesizes two distinct influences: the wind-carved geomorphic tectonics of Saudi Arabia’s coastal desert rock formations and the environmental wisdom of traditional Hejazi architecture. In historic Jeddah, the timber roshan (mashrabiya) screens of Al-Balad historically projected beyond the stone façade to shade glazing, induce natural stack ventilation, and filter intense desert glare—principles directly reinterpreted here through contemporary computational envelope design.

02 / Microclimatic Inputs

Reading environmental forces before shaping the mass

Jeddah experiences an arid subtropical marine climate characterized by intense solar radiation, high humidity, and prolonged summer periods where dry-bulb temperatures regularly exceed 45°C during standard business hours. Annual psychrometric data confirms that outdoor thermal comfort is restricted to the winter months between December and March, requiring aggressive passive cooling strategies for the rest of the year.

Microclimatic simulations identify east and west façade orientations as the most vulnerable to acute thermal shock and glare due to low-angle morning and afternoon sun angles. Prevailing wind vectors align along a persistent north-south axis. These environmental realities establish that envelope geometry cannot be treated as an arbitrary skin; rather, orientation, self-shading massing, and solar filtration must drive the building's volumetric form from the earliest design stage.

Environmental driver Solar radiation is the starting point for the tower’s form and envelope.

In Jeddah’s hot, humid coastal climate, the study focuses attention on the intense exposure received by glazing—especially at low-angle east and west sun—before the façade is designed.

03 / Statutory Envelope & Alignments

Translating planning codes into a computational design domain

Before initiating volumetric explorations, the site’s zoning parameters were translated into parametric boundary constraints. Statutory planning codes dictate maximum permissible ground coverage, stepped perimeter setbacks, and floor-area ratio (FAR) limitations, defining the maximal permissible geometric envelope.

An urban alignment analysis established primary setback lines along adjacent public right-of-ways and determined subterranean allowances for underground parking. This computational bounding box provided the precise domain within which all subsequent massing iterations were generated and evaluated.

04 / Parametric Option Space

Multi-objective exploration across eight architectural variants

Rather than developing an intuitive single scheme, an automated parametric option space was generated in Grasshopper. Eight distinct architectural massing topologies were systematically modeled by permuting three core variables: core placement (central spine, perimeter cores, or twin lateral cores), atrium morphology (continuous vertical shaft, open carved canyon, or enclosed glazed lightwell), and commercial podium distribution.

Each option was evaluated concurrently through quantitative real-estate yield and environmental criteria. A custom Grasshopper pipeline extracted total Built-Up Area (BUA) alongside typical-floor Daylight Factor (DF) distribution maps, establishing a multi-criteria decision matrix that allowed the team to evaluate trade-offs between rentable density and daylight autonomy.

05 / Selection Logic: Option 1 vs. Option 2

Balancing usable area, core efficiency and daylight penetration

Following multi-criteria evaluation, Option 1 was selected as the optimal architectural scheme. By splitting the office volume into two parallel wings flanking a central carved atrium canyon, Option 1 achieved the highest net-to-gross Built-Up Area while guaranteeing that all workspace zones receive abundant natural daylight from both outer perimeters and the protected internal atrium faces.

In contrast, Option 2 explored a continuous rectilinear block enclosed by a monumental perimeter structural diagrid that sheltered a central stepped atrium. While Option 2 provided dramatic interior spatiality, its deeper floorplates reduced daylight autonomy in middle workspace zones and resulted in lower net lettable efficiency. Option 1 was therefore advanced into full architectural and environmental development.

06 / Programmatic Section

Stratifying parking, public commerce and flexible workplace

The vertical section resolves a complex programmatic stack: integrating required vehicular parking infrastructure, an activated public commercial podium, and multi-tenant corporate offices above. Parking demand is calculated based on municipal standards of one parking stall per 45 m² of leasable office area (for an average 10,000 m² office programme), yielding a requirement of over 219 stalls.

To preserve valuable ground-level frontage for retail activity while minimizing excavation depth in coral-rich coastal ground, parking is stratified across three levels: Basement G-1 (130 stalls), Ground Floor (47 stalls), and Level 1 (42 stalls). The ground and first floors provide active commercial street frontages, double-height corporate lobbies, and food and beverage venues, above which the office floors rise around the central atrium.

07 / Tenancy Space Planning

One structural chassis supporting multiple leasing models

The office floorplates are engineered for maximum commercial leasing flexibility, allowing commercial tenants to occupy full floors or subdivide spaces with adaptable demountable partition systems. The typical floor plate comprises 1,080 m² of net usable office space, split into two 540 m² wings that flank the central atrium and are serviced by twin consolidated core blocks housing high-speed elevators, fire escape stairs, and MEP shafts.

A continuous 1.5-meter-wide circulation loop links both wings, providing seamless connectivity between work areas, central restrooms, and vertical cores. Three primary tenancy typologies were verified within the same structural grid:

2-TENANT (2 × 540 M²) → 4-TENANT (4 × 250–264 M²) → 6-TENANT (6 × 91–208 M²)

08 / Canyon Terraces & Biophilia

Carving the mass for views, shaded microclimates and human scale

The form-development methodology proceeds through a disciplined six-stage computational operation: establishing the urban site footprint, extruding the podium and tower volumes, carving dual central recesses inward to create open spaces, rotating masses to capture unobstructed views toward the Red Sea and surrounding cityscape, integrating solar self-shading geometries, and detailing the articulated façade envelope.

Within the central carved canyon, the building steps back floor by floor to create a dramatic series of curved landscaped sky terraces. These green decks act as outdoor communal breakout spaces and informal collaborative zones, introducing shade and planted outdoor space into the heart of the office environment.

09 / Phyllotactic Façade & Solar Self-Shading

Biomimetic phyllotaxis for passive solar radiation control

To shield expansive glass surfaces from extreme heat gain without compromising office daylight autonomy, the envelope takes inspiration from phyllotaxis—the geometric spiral arrangement of leaves and scales found in botanical structures. In nature, phyllotactic angles optimize solar capture; here, the geometric logic is inverted to produce continuous self-shading, where protruding, faceted cassette fins naturally cast protective shadows across adjacent glazing as the sun traverses the sky.

Seasonal radiation simulations conducted across four critical solar benchmarks—the Summer Solstice (21 June), Autumnal Equinox (23 September), Vernal Equinox (21 March), and Winter Solstice (22 December) at 09:00, 12:00, and 15:00—demonstrate dramatic insolation reductions on all four building corners (East, North, West, and South). Incident radiation drops substantially when comparing flat glazed envelopes [1] against the articulated self-shading cassettes [2]. These comparisons inform the shading proposal; they do not establish measured cooling-energy savings.

The façade module is constructed from precision-folded, perforated metallic panels that alternate with narrow, high-performance double-glazed vision strips. The calibrated perforations admit soft, indirect ambient light while deflecting direct radiant heat, with the intention of supporting daylight while reducing direct solar exposure.

Read each pair on the same radiation scale: blue indicates lower incident solar energy and red indicates higher exposure. These are façade radiation simulations, not humidity maps or measured cooling-energy savings.

10 / Materiality & Presence

Civic identity at pedestrian scale and along the skyline

The completed architectural proposal synthesizes rigorous environmental engineering with an expressive civic presence. At street level, the double-height commercial podium establishes an active urban edge flanked by drought-tolerant desert landscaping, water features, and shaded pedestrian promenades that anchor the development within its district.

At night, the tower’s character shifts from an opaque, sculptural desert monolith to a crystalline, glowing beacon. Interior lighting filters through the perforated metal skin, accentuating the rhythm of the folded envelope while casting a soft ambient glow across the central planted canyon—establishing a memorable nocturnal landmark along northern Jeddah’s highway corridor.

Reflection

Arwad Tower demonstrates how performance-driven computational design bridges the gap between commercial viability and environmental rigor in extreme desert climates. By integrating parametric massing catalogues, daylight factor simulations, tenancy space planning, and bio-inspired self-shading envelopes into a coherent methodological sequence, the project achieves an energy-conscious commercial architecture where environmental performance directly informs expressive tectonic identity.
