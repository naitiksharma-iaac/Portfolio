Post Digital Temples investigates whether recurrent geometric characteristics of Indian temple spires can be encoded parametrically and learned through a three-dimensional generative model.

The study analyses two broad temple traditions, generates controlled synthetic datasets, voxelises the forms and uses GAN experiments to test what morphological characteristics can be learned and recombined.

A geometric rather than exhaustive cultural study

The computational focus is limited to recurrent three-dimensional characteristics of temple spires. It does not claim to reproduce every historical or cultural dimension of temple architecture.

Controlled generated samples

Generative Grasshopper definitions produced multiple spire variations while controlling their geometric features, creating sufficient samples for machine-learning experiments without relying only on scanned monuments.

Parametric generation and learned form

The animations trace the project’s development from plan and profile variations to a family of three-dimensional forms and a learned model.
