Charge Pavilion

Institution
IAAC
Programme
MaCAD
Term
Winter 2021
Location
Davao City, Philippines

Charge Pavilion combines minimal surfaces and differential growth to generate a continuous, environmentally responsive pavilion in Davao City.

Minimal-surface form finding establishes the roof-column geometry, while environmental optimisation, structural analysis and a differential-growth shading layer respond to a hot tropical climate.

A minimal surface attempts to minimise area within its boundary conditions. In the computational workflow, mesh topology, anchor points and relaxation goals produce a continuous geometry in which roof and column conditions merge.

Connected nodes remain close through attraction, nearby nodes maintain spacing through repulsion, and alignment regulates connected neighbours. Repeated iterations produce a branching and folding shading layer rather than random ornament.

Direct-radiation analysis identifies Davao City as generally hot through the year. The pavilion therefore combines vehicle charging with shaded occupied space and cooling opportunities.

Galapagos evolutionary optimisation was used to study structural height in relation to reducing solar radiation and increasing shaded area. It is not described as AI optimisation.

Displacement, utilisation, principal stress lines and material distribution were compared to locate high-demand regions, lower-demand regions and possible areas for material redistribution.

Principal-stress analysis identifies high-demand and lower-demand regions and helps establish where material may be reduced or redistributed.
