My contribution

Keeping the design intact,
from digital curve to cast GFRC.

I worked independently on the fabrication development of the curved glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) profiles at Studio Symbiosis. I handled the geometry, parametric workflow and fabrication drawings, coordinating directly with the façade consultant, the GFRC contractor and the engineering team on site.

My responsibility was to ensure that the contractor’s factory could reproduce the intended design geometry without deviation, and that the same profiles could be carried through to installation.

01 / The design to preserve

A curved form opening
towards the mountains.

Studio Symbiosis shaped the building to open towards the Langtang mountain range. The curved plan and projecting terraces establish the form; the continuous bands give that geometry a strong expression across the façade.

The studio’s design also draws on Kathmandu’s prayer flags through its coloured vertical glass fins. My work focused on the curved GFRC profiles: translating this architectural intent into components the contractor could fabricate and install.

Site context and original design study

02 / Geometry into components

One continuous gesture.
Many different profiles.

The challenge was to preserve the changing curvature when the continuous bands were divided into manufacturable pieces. I developed the parametric geometry and component divisions, using the curtain-wall grid as a reference. Typical divisions followed two glass panels, with further subdivisions for production, handling and transport.

I coordinated the geometric relationship between the GFRC, its supports and the curtain wall with the façade consultant, contractor and site engineers. The model gave us a common reference for discussing changes without losing the intended profile.

Developing the bands parametrically

The model links the overall band geometry to its local component conditions. These recordings show the digital process behind the fabrication work.

03 / Drawing automation

One script.
A family of fabrication drawings.

I developed a script to generate the fabrication drawings for the different band geometries. Each output carried its own component reference, ordered rib profiles and assembly information, so the factory could reconstruct the intended curvature from a consistent set of instructions.

The five examples below come from the band fabrication package. Their profiles change, but the drawing logic remains the same: identify the band, locate it in the overall assembly, label its ribs and show how adjacent parts meet.

Select a fabrication drawing to inspect the labels at full resolution.

04 / At the GFRC contractor’s factory

Making the digital
geometry buildable.

My work continued beyond issuing drawings. I coordinated with the GFRC contractor and reviewed the physical profiles at the factory, checking how the intended geometry was being translated into moulds and cast pieces.

The labelled rib framework makes the curved surface tangible. Reviewing the assembled profiles and the joints between adjacent pieces was essential to carrying the design through fabrication without geometric deviation.

05 / Installation in Kathmandu

Following the geometry
all the way to site.

I visited Kathmandu every month to review installation and coordinate with the site engineering team, façade consultant and GFRC contractor. The initial typical bands established how the profiles would be installed; once that method was in place, the site engineers managed the continuing work.

Site conditions also required practical local solutions. Full-scale layouts and physical templates helped translate the geometry into something the team could check and work with on the ground, alongside the digital model and fabrication drawings.
